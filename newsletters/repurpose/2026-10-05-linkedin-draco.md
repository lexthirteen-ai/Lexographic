## POST

Every morning my chief of staff hands me one page.

The one thing. What rolled over. What kind of day it is. One thing to leave alone.

I tick the boxes and do the work. It is 300 words, it is written before I wake up, and it has never once sent anything.

Here's what is actually on it (steal the whole shape):

🎯 **The one thing.** Not a list. One. And it has to come from a plan I already ticked, so it can't invent a priority overnight.

🔁 **Rolled over.** What I said I'd do and didn't. After three briefs it stops being polite and asks me to tick it or tell it it's a no.

🎩 **Today's hat.** Which version of the job today is. Maker, operator, or in front of people. Everything else reads differently once that's named.

📅 **The shape.** Today's calls, and anything I already promised the person on the other end. That last part is the one that saves me.

🙋 **Needs you.** The things that stall without a human. Named, so they stop hiding.

💷 **Pipeline and money.** What is coming in, what is going out.

🚫 **Don't do today.** A short list of good ideas I am saying no to, in writing, so I stop relitigating them at 4pm.

Then one line per input with its date, because a stale calendar is a finding, not a detail. If something is missing it goes at the top. Absence is information.

The part I'd steal first:

**It reads everything so I read one page.** Seven other stars do the work underneath, and Draco's whole job is to turn that into something I can hold in my head before coffee. It does none of the work itself. That is the design, not a limitation.

And the rule that makes the rest of it safe to run: nothing a star touches is ever sent, booked, posted, paid or scheduled. One job per star, one human gate, and a box only I tick.

Most people automating their day hand over the deciding and keep the typing. I did it the other way round.

The whole team ships free as charters you can read and edit, with the morning brief working out of the box. You need Obsidian and about ten minutes.

What's the first thing you look at in the morning, and did you choose it?

## Framework map

- **Step 1, Target:** solo operators and small studios whose day gets set by whoever emailed first.
- **Step 2, Collective Fear:** reacting all day and never choosing, then finding the year gone.
- **Step 3, Message Fear Implicitly:** "did you choose it?" in the closer, and the rollover section doing the work earlier.
- **Step 4, Tease the Solve:** "one page," in the opening line.
- **Step 5, Amplify:** three briefs of rollover, and relitigating the same good idea at 4pm.
- **Step 6, Demonstrate:** the seven sections, concrete and free.

**Fear identified:** losing authorship of your own day, one reasonable interruption at a time.
